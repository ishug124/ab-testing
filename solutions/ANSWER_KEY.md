# Suggested answers and hints

Try the workbook activities before reading this page. More than one answer may be reasonable.

- **00 Getting started:** Three conversions out of five people is 60%. Changing a zero to one adds one person divided by the group size.
- **01 Random assignment:** Even with equal true rates, observed rates usually differ a little by chance. Randomization balances groups on average, not perfectly in every sample.
- **02 Metrics:** Pooled click rate weights people with many views more. Average personal rate gives each person equal weight. They answer different questions.
- **03 Effects:** Absolute change is treatment minus control. Relative lift divides that change by control. A p-value is calculated under a stated no-effect model; it is not the chance the treatment works.
- **04 Power:** A smaller MDE is harder to notice, so it needs more participants. Do not lower the evidence bar after seeing results.
- **05 Trust checks:** SRM is a reason to pause and investigate duplicates, redirects, eligibility, missing events, and bot filters.
- **06 Peeking:** Repeated looks can increase false alarms for fixed-end methods. Plan a fixed end point or use a sequential method chosen in advance.
- **07 CUPED:** Use a useful pre-period measure because treatment could not have changed it. Do not adjust for a behavior caused by treatment.
- **08 Designs:** Cluster assignment reduces spillovers. Switchbacks need attention to carryover. Difference-in-differences needs a parallel-trends assumption.
- **09 Capstone:** A good recommendation includes effect size, uncertainty, counts, guardrail, practical threshold, validity checks, and a next step.
- **10 Bayesian and bandits:** A prior contributes a starting distribution. Bandits can optimize short-term allocation; fixed tests often give clearer causal comparisons and guardrail monitoring.
