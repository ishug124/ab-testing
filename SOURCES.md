# Sources and further learning

The supplied notes suggested Udacity, Ron Kohavi's practical experimentation courses, a Bayesian Python course, Coursera, CXL, Data36, freeCodeCamp, and Causal Inference: The Mixtape. Treat these as leads; course details and availability can change.

## Research and statistical guidance

- American Statistical Association, [Statement on Statistical Significance and P-Values](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf).
- Deng, Xu, Kohavi, and Walker (2013), [CUPED paper](https://ai.stanford.edu/~ronnyk/2013-02CUPEDImprovingSensitivityOfControlledExperiments.pdf).
- Johari, Pekelis, and Walsh (2015), [Always Valid Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.04922).
- Kohavi, Deng, and Vermeer (2022), [A/B Testing Intuition Busters](https://exp-platform.com/abtestingintuitionbusters/).
- Blanchet et al. (2026), [Conservative Inference in Switchback Experiments](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/blanchet26a.html).
- Holtz et al., [Interference and cluster randomization in marketplace experiments](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.01157).

## Official software guides

- Google, [Welcome to Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/notebooks/intro.ipynb).
- NumPy, [Random Generator](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/random/generator.html).
- SciPy, [Independent-sample t-test](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.ttest_ind.html).
- SciPy, [Chi-square goodness-of-fit test](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.chisquare.html).

All workbook examples use simulated data, not real company or user records.
